A book you loved, read once and shelved forever, is a small tragedy.

Multiply it by every shelf in your city and you have a library the size of a warehouse — locked, unlit, and quietly forgotten.

Read Forward turns the lights on.

The whole of it

The complete reading lifecycle. One ecosystem.

Most reading apps pick a single job and stop there — a catalogue, or a tracker, or a forum. Readers don't live in one of those. You find a book. You finish a book. You keep going. You tell someone. And then it begins again, with the next one. Read Forward is built the way reading actually happens.

Lend & Borrow

Your bookshelf, quietly working.

Turn around and count the books you've already finished. Every one of them is doing nothing. Three streets away, someone is putting off buying that exact title. Read Forward puts the two of you in the same room — and it costs you the walk to the café where you hand it over.

  • Nearest first, always. The feed is ranked by real distance, and every card tells you how far — 700 m, not "in your area".
  • Listing takes seconds. Point your camera at the barcode. Cover, author and blurb arrive on their own.
  • Nobody loses a book. A one-time code seals the handover, so both of you always know exactly where it is.
  • Then it keeps going. Finished a borrowed book? Send it straight to the next reader instead of back to the shelf it came from.

Reading Habit

Reading doesn't fail on ambition.
It fails on Tuesdays.

Nobody abandons a book on the first night. It goes quietly, on an ordinary evening, somewhere around page forty. Read Forward's only job is to make the next four pages the easiest thing you do today — and then to show you the receipts, night after night, until stopping feels worse than continuing.

  • One swipe to log. Pages or percentage — whichever way you actually think about a book.
  • A streak you can see. Seven days at a glance, the full month behind it, your longest run marked.
  • Reminders that read the room. Pick your hour. Read before it, and the nudge never arrives.
  • A month, summarised. Days read, pages turned, books closed. Quiet, honest, and nobody's business but yours.

Ask someone what they read last year and watch them struggle. Not because it didn't matter — because nothing was keeping count. What you're reading now sits at the top. What you've closed stays below, and by December it's a list you'll want to show someone.

Communities

Reading is a solitary act.
It was never meant to be a lonely one.

The best part of a book is the hour afterwards, when you find someone who has also read it. Read Forward isn't built only for readers — it's built for the groups they belong to. The Saturday club. The campus society. The neighbourhood library. Each gets a home of its own, and together they become something none of them could be alone: a city of readers who can actually find each other.

  • A home, not a group chat. Members, shelves, and everything your community is reading — in one place that doesn't scroll away by morning.
  • Evenings worth turning up to. Meetups, author sessions, read-alongs and swaps, with RSVPs so you know who's coming.
  • Arguments about chapter seven. Threads that live under the book itself, so the good discussion is still there in a year.
  • Reading as a team sport. Group challenges and shared milestones — because the streak is easier when eighty people are on it with you.
  • A shelf you all own. Members lend to each other first, and the club's books stay in circulation instead of in cupboards.

In-app currency

Generosity, gently kept score of.

LitCoins are what a lending community runs on instead of money. Give a book to someone who needed it and you've earned the right to ask for one back. That's the entire economy — and no real money ever touches it.

  • Earned every time a book of yours ends up in the right hands.
  • Spent when it's your turn to ask.
  • Awarded for finishing a book, and for streaks that hold.
  • Shared — bring a friend in and you both start ahead.
